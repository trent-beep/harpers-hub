# Casework Charter

**Purpose.** Hold the file. For long-term missing persons cases with active public
appeals, this project maintains a disciplined, fully-sourced public record, keeps
cases visible, and generates carefully-vetted leads for official channels — so that
no case goes cold simply because nobody was still reading the file.

This charter is binding on every agent and every human contributor. Where any task
instruction conflicts with this charter, **the charter wins**.

---

## Hard rules

1. **Public record only.** We work exclusively from information that is already
   public: official registers (missingpersons.gov.au, NSW Police), government media
   releases, coronial findings, court records, and reputable news reporting. We never
   access, solicit, or store non-public information. No pretexting, no scraping of
   private accounts, no approaches to witnesses or anyone connected to a case.

2. **We never identify suspects.** No artifact in this project may name, describe
   identifiably, or imply the identity of any suspect or person of interest — even
   where a news outlet has published a name. Refer generically only ("a person of
   interest was examined at the inquest"). The lesson of the Boston Marathon
   misidentification is written into this rule. No exceptions.

3. **Leads go to official channels, never to the public.** Anything we develop is
   handed to police (via Crime Stoppers 1800 333 000 or a named strike force), the
   Coroner, or the family's legal/advocacy representatives. We do not publish
   theories. We do not operate a tip line — anyone offering information is directed
   to Crime Stoppers immediately and verbatim.

4. **Provenance or it doesn't exist.** Every fact carries its source (outlet/agency,
   date, URL). A claim without provenance is not recorded as fact. Memory is not a
   source. AI recall is not a source.

5. **Families lead.** Deep analysis on a case happens only with the engagement of
   the family or their chosen advocates (e.g. The Disappeared Project). Without that,
   we limit ourselves to compiling the public record and amplifying the official
   appeal. Awareness material about a person is subject to their family's veto at
   all times.

6. **Cultural safety.** For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, follow
   community protocols: care with names and images of people who may have died
   (display warnings where appropriate), family and community consent for any
   material, and preference for partnering with First Nations-led organisations.

7. **Do no harm to the person or the investigation.** Some missing people are alive
   and have chosen not to be found; if a person is located, their privacy prevails —
   the record closes with "located" and nothing more. We publish nothing that could
   compromise a future prosecution, and we defer to police requests to withhold.

8. **Adversarial review before anything leaves.** No lead, brief, or public page
   leaves this repository until it has survived the adversarial gate (see
   PIPELINE.md) **and** been signed off by the responsible human. Agents never
   publish, post, email, or transmit anything externally on their own.

9. **Honest limits.** We are a support and persistence layer, not detectives, not
   police, and we say so plainly in every external artifact. We claim no authority
   and imply none.

## Case selection criteria

A case may be opened here only if **all** of the following hold:

- The person has been missing long-term (indicatively 2+ years), and
- An official public appeal exists (police/AFP register entry, standing reward, or
  coronial referral), and
- Working the case publicly cannot plausibly endanger the missing person (excludes
  family-violence flight, protected persons, and recent disappearances of minors).

Standing government rewards and completed inquests are strong signals: they mean
the state itself is actively asking the public to engage with the case.

## Tone

These are real people and living families, not content. Write every artifact as if
the family will read it, because they may.
