## Snapshot

Robyn Hickie, aged 18, a dental nurse, was last seen about 7.15pm on Saturday
7 April 1979, standing at a bus stop on the Pacific Highway, Belmont North
(Lake Macquarie, NSW). (Sources: NSW Police reward page, accessed Aug 2026;
Newcastle Herald, "'I hope to God they get answers'")

A physical description was not reproducible from retrievable allowed-source
text (UNVERIFIED here); her photograph and description appear on the NSW Police
reward page and AFP register profile.

## Timeline

- 1979-04-07 (evening) — At home, Robyn received a phone call inviting her to a
  party. She did not expect to be long and promised to return home by 11pm.
  She left to catch a bus. [OFFICIAL] (Source: AFP register profile text)
- 1979-04-07 ~19:15 — Last seen standing at a bus stop on the Pacific Highway,
  Belmont North. [OFFICIAL] (Source: NSW Police reward page)
- Despite extensive investigations at the time and over the years, she has
  never been located. [OFFICIAL] (Source: NSW Police reward page)
- 1979-04-21 — Thirteen days later, Amanda Robinson (14) disappeared in the
  same district (see `../amanda-robinson/case-file.md`). [REPORTED] (Source:
  Newcastle Herald, "Decades of suspended grief for loved ones")

## Investigation history

- 1979 — Original investigation later found by the coroner to be "ad hoc,
  unstructured and dysfunctional": failures to consider links between the three
  disappearances, to take statements establishing alibis for persons of
  interest, and to follow up leads; the criticism prompted a NSW Police apology
  to the families. (Source: Newcastle Herald, "Fresh inquiry…", 2025)
- c. 1998 — Within Strike Force Fenwick's re-investigation. (Sources: NSW
  Parliament Q&A 4450; Submission 63)
- 2001–2002 — Joint coronial inquest before State Coroner John Abernethy;
  findings July 2002: deceased, most likely as a result of foul play — murdered
  by persons unknown. (Sources: NSW Police reward page; NSW Parliament
  Submission 63)
- 2019-04 — Strike Force Arapaima established to re-examine this case with the
  Robinson and Kotevski cases. (Sources: NSW Police reward page; ABC News,
  25 Nov 2019)
- 2019-11 — Green mid-1970s four-door Holden Torana sedan named publicly as a
  "significant" possible lead for the Hickie and Robinson cases (Det Ch Insp
  Greg Thomas). (Sources: ABC News, 25 Nov 2019; Newcastle Herald, Nov 2019)
- 2021-08-16 — $1,000,000 reward announced; Det Insp Steve Benson said
  investigators were following "strong lines of inquiry" and hoped the reward
  would "entice those people in the community that still have information".
  (Sources: ABC News, 16 Aug 2021; Newcastle Herald, Aug 2021)
- 2021-08 — Arapaima search of the former Camp Kanangra scout camp site, Nords
  Wharf. (Source: Newcastle Herald, Aug 2021)
- 2025-10 — Era raised at the NSW Legislative Council inquiry into unsolved
  murders and long-term missing persons 1965–2010. (Source: NSW Parliament
  inquiry page)

## What police ask the public

- $1,000,000 reward on offer for information regarding the disappearance and
  suspected murder. (Source: NSW Police reward page)
- Specific standing ask: information about a green mid-1970s four-door Holden
  Torana sedan possibly linked to the Hickie and Robinson disappearances.
  (Sources: ABC News, 25 Nov 2019; Newcastle Herald, Nov 2019)
- Anyone with information: **Crime Stoppers 1800 333 000** or the Crime
  Stoppers online reporting page. (Source: NSW Police reward page)

## Family advocacy

- Jim Hickie (father) campaigned publicly for decades; at the 2021 reward
  announcement, aged 91: "if we're going to get answers, it has to be soon
  doesn't it, I won't last forever". (Sources: ABC News, 16 Aug 2021; Newcastle
  Herald, "Decades of suspended grief for loved ones")

## Open questions on the record

1. The green Torana remains a police-named open lead — publicly unresolved in
   the record reviewed. (Sources: ABC News, 25 Nov 2019)
2. The inquest's documented investigative failures (statements never taken,
   leads not followed) define information the record shows was never captured.
   (Sources: Newcastle Herald, "Fresh inquiry…", 2025; NSW Parliament
   Submission 63)
