# Linked record: the Eastlakes disappearances, 1978–79

Shared context for the case files of **Leanne Goodall**, **Robyn Hickie** and
**Amanda Robinson** — three young women who disappeared within about four months
of each other (December 1978 – April 1979) in the Newcastle / Lake Macquarie
(Eastlakes) area, examined together at a joint coronial inquest and
re-investigated by successive strike forces. Compiled 2026-08-26.

## What officially connects the cases

- All three lived in the Eastlakes area; all were young; all went missing at
  night while alone. (Source: Newcastle Herald, "Decades of suspended grief for
  loved ones")
- The coroner found police at the time failed to consider a possible link
  between the three disappearances despite their closeness in time and place.
  (Source: Newcastle Herald, "Fresh inquiry could answer decades-old missing
  persons mysteries", 2025)

## Strike Force Fenwick (c. 1998)

Established to investigate the disappearance of up to 20 young people from the
greater Newcastle region over a 20-year period; its caseload is documented in a
NSW Parliament Q&A record. (Source: NSW Parliament, Legislative Council Q&A
No 4450) Journalistic research into the Goodall, Robinson, Hickie and other
Hunter disappearances from early 1998 is documented in a submission published
by NSW Parliament. (Source: NSW Parliament Submission 63, N. Mercer, 2025)

## The joint inquest (2001 → findings July 2002)

- NSW State Coroner John Abernethy examined the three cases together at an
  inquest held at Toronto NSW, beginning 2001, findings delivered July 2002: the
  three women had been murdered by persons unknown. (Sources: NSW Parliament
  Submission 63; Newcastle Herald story 495179 — headline withheld from this
  record because it contains the name of a person of interest)
- Persons of interest were examined during the inquest; consistent with the
  Charter, none is identified anywhere in this project.
- **Documented shortcomings of the original investigations:** the coroner found
  police failed to obtain written statements from important witnesses, failed to
  carry out follow-up inquiries, failed to take statements from persons of
  interest to establish alibis, and — in the Goodall case — launched no
  investigation at all; he remarked that detectives should have analysed the
  cases after two years and referred them to a coroner. (Source: NSW Parliament
  Submission 63) He described the original investigations as "ad hoc,
  unstructured and dysfunctional", criticism that prompted a NSW Police apology
  to the families. (Source: Newcastle Herald, "Fresh inquiry…", 2025)

## Strike Force Arapaima (2019) and later activity

- 2019-04 — Lake Macquarie detectives established Strike Force Arapaima to
  re-examine the Hickie and Robinson cases together with the 1994 disappearance
  of Gordana Kotevski (see `../gordana-kotevski/case-file.md`). (Sources: NSW
  Police reward pages; ABC News, 25 Nov 2019)
- 2019-11 — Police released an image of a green mid-1970s four-door Holden
  Torana sedan; Det Ch Insp Greg Thomas described it as a "significant" lead
  possibly linked to the Hickie and Robinson cases. (Sources: ABC News,
  25 Nov 2019; Newcastle Herald, Nov 2019)
- 2021-08-16 — $1,000,000 rewards announced for Hickie and Robinson; Police
  Minister David Elliott: "If your conscience won't make you act, maybe
  $2 million will." (Source: ABC News, 16 Aug 2021)
- 2021-08 — Arapaima detectives searched the former Camp Kanangra scout camp
  site at Nords Wharf. (Source: Newcastle Herald, Aug 2021)
- 2025-10-15 — NSW Legislative Council established an inquiry into unsolved
  murders and long-term missing persons cases in NSW 1965–2010, with these cases
  among those raised. (Sources: NSW Parliament inquiry page; Newcastle Herald,
  "Fresh inquiry…", 2025)
